Privacy

{{BRAND}} runs on the Tari™ Ootle testnet. What we collect, what we can't, every table in our database, and how long each thing lasts.

The short version

  • We collect ciphertext, padded sizes, hours, rules, open counts, your drive's random id and a hash of its login key (if you use one), and invoices for space you rent. The full list is below.
  • We don't collect names, emails, phone numbers, file names, file contents, keys, passwords, analytics or tracking cookies.
  • Your IP: our server sees your IP address while you're connected. We don't write it to disk: rate limits use a keyed hash, kept in memory, with a key that changes every day. Our web server keeps no access logs.
  • Third parties: our host, Hostinger, which can see what our server stores; Let's Encrypt, for our certificate; Tari's indexer, only when you press “Check it on Tari's indexer”. No analytics, no trackers, no third-party scripts or fonts.

Your IP address

We need your IP address to answer you, and we see it while you're connected. We don't write it to disk. To rate-limit, we keep a keyed hash of your network prefix in memory, with a key that is regenerated every day at 00:00 UTC and never stored. Our web server writes no access log, and its error log drops addresses, paths and headers. A VPN or Tor hides your IP from us.

What we publish

Our live numbers (/api/v1/stats) are public: the free quota; our disk's size and free space, rounded; whether uploads or the Tari features are paused; how many drives were active recently, once there are enough of them; SealLog's latest checkpoint; and how many minutes ago the latest file finished uploading, to the minute.

Our public endpoint /api/v1/stats/wall lists, for up to 24 recent files at least an hour old, a coarse size and an exit rule, only when at least 8 exist in the last week: never a name, an id or whether a file was opened. Someone who knows a file's size and rule may still recognise it there. Our hourly test file and our own test transactions are public too; never a visitor's.

How long things last

We delete the ciphertext from our disk within a minute of its exit. If the exit was a download, we wait for it to finish. We never had the key, so any leftover bytes are noise.

Our database (never files) is backed up daily, and each copy is deleted after . It holds the rows listed above, including the wrapped key of every link still open when the copy was made; a wrapped key opens nothing without the link's secret. What we erase from the database is overwritten, but an older copy of it can sit in the database's write-ahead log until that space is reused, and in the backups until they're deleted.

Our host (Hostinger) can take weekly snapshots of the whole server. We haven't switched them off, and we haven't yet confirmed in its panel how long it keeps them. A file that existed when one was taken stays in it, as ciphertext, until that snapshot is rotated out.

Our host doesn't keep snapshots of the server.

Disks can't be securely wiped, so deleted ciphertext can linger in free blocks. Without the key it's noise.

Deleting everything

Delete a link at any time from the card you got when you made it, or from your drive. Delete my drive, in your drive, removes its record, its files and every link to them; it asks for your key first.

Your browser

We set no cookies. Your drive key lives in your browser's own storage (IndexedDB), and small preferences, like Lights off, in localStorage. Nothing in them is sent to us except what each action needs.

Your browser history, and any history sync, can keep the full link, key included. We clear the key from the address bar once it's opened, but we can't reach your history.

Third parties

  • Hostinger rents us the server. It can see everything our server stores, and its snapshots are described above.
  • Let's Encrypt issues our certificate. It never sees our visitors.
  • Tari's public indexer, only when you press “Check it on Tari's indexer”. It then sees your IP address. Otherwise your browser talks only to this site.

Limits and space

Free space is a fair share of one real disk, recalculated every hour, and it can go down. When it does, nothing of yours is deleted, but you can't add more until you're under it again. If the disk truly fills, new uploads pause for everyone. Rented space adds to your share for the period you paid for. The live limits are on Pricing and limits

Your drive and its key

Your drive opens with a key your browser makes; there is no email and no password with us. Your recovery code or key file is the only way back into it. If you lose it, nobody, including us, can let you back in, and your kept files are lost with it. Anyone who has it can open your drive, so keep it private.

Deletion, expiry and the end of extra space

A file leaves your drive when you delete it, when the trash is emptied after days, at the time you set with Delete it after, or after the last open of a link set to delete it from your drive. A link that ends on a file you keep only ends the link. When a file leaves, we delete the ciphertext within a minute (after a download in progress finishes).

When extra space ends, nothing goes for days. Then files in the trash go first, then the oldest, until you fit.

If you don't open your drive for days, we delete what's in it.

Removal

We can remove any link, file or drop by its id, without seeing inside, when it's reported and we believe it breaks these terms, or when it's abusing limits. We usually disable first and ask questions later.

What we can hand over

If compelled by law, we can hand over what we have: ciphertext, the columns listed on our privacy page, and invoices. We can't hand over contents or keys, because we never have them.
